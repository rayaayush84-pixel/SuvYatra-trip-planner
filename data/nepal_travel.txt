BEST TIME TO VISIT NEPAL
The best seasons are autumn (October to November) and spring (March to April). Autumn has clear skies and stable weather, ideal for trekking. Spring has blooming rhododendrons and warm days. Monsoon runs roughly June to August with heavy rain, leeches on trails and cloudy mountain views. Winter (December to February) is cold at altitude but clear; lowland areas like Chitwan and Pokhara are pleasant.

VISA AND ENTRY
Most nationalities can get a tourist visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu or at land border crossings. Typical durations are 15, 30 and 90 days, priced around USD 30, 50 and 125. Indian citizens do not need a visa. Bring a passport valid for at least six months and passport photos. Always check the Department of Immigration website for current rules and fees.

CURRENCY AND MONEY
The currency is the Nepalese Rupee (NPR). ATMs are common in Kathmandu, Pokhara and big towns, but scarce on trekking routes, so carry enough cash for treks. Cards are accepted in upmarket hotels and restaurants. Bargaining is normal in tourist markets.

KATHMANDU VALLEY
Kathmandu has three Durbar Squares (Kathmandu, Patan, Bhaktapur), all UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Other must-see places are Swayambhunath (Monkey Temple), Boudhanath Stupa, Pashupatinath Temple and Thamel for shopping and food. Bhaktapur is known for medieval Newar architecture, pottery and juju dhau (king curd). Nagarkot and Chandragiri offer mountain views near the valley.

POKHARA
Pokhara is the lakeside gateway to the Annapurna region. Popular activities are boating on Phewa Lake, sunrise at Sarangkot, paragliding, the World Peace Pagoda, Davis Falls, Gupteshwor Cave and the Mahendra Cave. Many trekkers start and end their Annapurna treks here. It is about a 6 to 8 hour drive from Kathmandu, or a 25 minute flight.

TREKKING - EVEREST REGION
Everest Base Camp trek takes about 12 to 14 days round trip, usually starting with a flight to Lukla. Highlights are Namche Bazaar, Tengboche Monastery, Kala Patthar viewpoint and Everest Base Camp at about 5,364 m. Permits needed include the Sagarmatha National Park permit and the local municipality permit. Gokyo Lakes and Three Passes are longer alternatives.

TREKKING - ANNAPURNA REGION
The Annapurna Circuit takes about 12 to 20 days and crosses Thorong La Pass at 5,416 m. Annapurna Base Camp takes about 7 to 10 days. Poon Hill (Ghorepani) is a short 4 to 5 day trek, great for beginners with sunrise Himalayan views. Permits: Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP). Mardi Himal is a quieter, shorter option.

TREKKING - OTHER REGIONS
Langtang Valley is close to Kathmandu and takes about 7 to 10 days. Manaslu Circuit and Upper Mustang need a restricted area permit and a licensed guide with a group. Everest, Annapurna and Langtang can be done independently, but rules change, so check with the Nepal Tourism Board or a registered trekking agency.

ALTITUDE SICKNESS AND SAFETY
Ascend slowly, ideally no more than 500 m of sleeping altitude gain per day above 3,000 m, and take rest days. Drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol at altitude, and descend immediately if symptoms like severe headache, vomiting or confusion appear. Buy travel insurance that covers helicopter evacuation up to 5,000 m or higher.

WILDLIFE - CHITWAN AND BARDIA
Chitwan National Park in the Terai lowlands offers jungle safaris to see one-horned rhinoceros, Bengal tigers (rare), elephants, gharial crocodiles and hundreds of bird species. Bardia National Park is quieter and better for tiger sightings. Best time is October to March. Most people stay in Sauraha for Chitwan.

LUMBINI AND SPIRITUAL SITES
Lumbini, in the Terai, is the birthplace of Lord Buddha and a UNESCO World Heritage Site with the Maya Devi Temple and many international monasteries. Muktinath, Janakpur (Janaki Mandir) and Pashupatinath are important Hindu pilgrimage places. Kathmandu also has many Buddhist monasteries, including Kopan and Boudhanath area gompas.

FOOD
Try dal bhat (rice with lentil soup and curry, often unlimited refills), momo (dumplings), thukpa (noodle soup), sel roti (rice doughnut), chatamari (Newari rice pizza), yomari, and Newari khaja sets. Drinks include masala tea, lassi and local raksi and tongba (millet beer). Drink only bottled, boiled or filtered water.

TRANSPORT
Domestic flights connect Kathmandu with Pokhara, Lukla, Bhairahawa and others; weather delays are common, so keep spare days. Tourist buses run Kathmandu to Pokhara and Chitwan. Local transport includes microbuses, taxis and ride apps like Pathao and InDrive in Kathmandu. Roads in the hills can be slow, especially in monsoon.

TRAVEL TIPS AND CULTURE
Dress modestly at temples, remove shoes before entering, and walk clockwise around stupas. Ask before photographing people. Greet with "Namaste". Nepal uses 230V power with plug types C, D and M. Buy a local SIM (Ncell or Nepal Telecom) at the airport with your passport. Major festivals include Dashain (Sept-Oct), Tihar (Oct-Nov), Holi (Feb-Mar) and Buddha Jayanti (Apr-May).

BUDGET
Budget travelers can manage roughly USD 25 to 40 per day in cities, with tea-house trekking costing similar amounts. Mid-range travel is about USD 60 to 120 per day. Prices vary by season, so treat these as rough estimates.
